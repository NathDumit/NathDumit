Stop 1½ · quick horror class

Relational × NoSQL × my DBF

Relational (SQL)

Tables linked by keys. The database itself guarantees that every order points to a customer that exists.

Postgres · SQL Server · MySQL

Non-relational (NoSQL)

Documents, key-value or graphs. Each record carries what it needs. Flexible and good at scaling.

MongoDB · Redis · Neo4j

The house's DBF

It looks relational: it has tables and columns. But no link is declared anywhere. Whoever knew how to join them was the program, and whoever knew the program.

dBase format, from the 80s

Relational is a registry office: every family tie on record. NoSQL is everyone carrying their own birth certificate in a bag. This DBF was an album of 652 photos with no captions.

Dados in Rio · Halloween · Nathália Dumit

For anyone outside the field, a thirty-second class to understand the size of the scare. In a relational database, tables are linked by keys, and the database itself guarantees those links: there is no order from a customer that does not exist. It is a registry office, every family tie is on record. In non-relational databases, NoSQL, each record carries what it needs, like a document; it is flexible and scales well. Now, what I found: DBF files, a format from the 80s. It looks relational, it has tables and columns, but no link is declared anywhere. Who knew how to connect one table to another was the program, and the person who knew the program. It is a family album with 652 photos and no captions. And nobody to ask who is who.

Stop 2 · the scare

Dados in Rio · Halloween · Nathália Dumit

Before any analysis, I needed to know what existed. I read every table file in the folder: 652. Of those, 493 were copies of other structures, leaving 159 distinct tables. And 82 of them had no column that could serve as a key, the one that identifies each row. Without a key, you cannot even tell how to link one table to another. This is where I screamed inside.

Stop 4 · the exorcism

Let the data say who links to whom

1

Read

Each table, with a profile of each column.

2

Find the key

A column, or combination, that never repeats.

3

Test the links

How many values of A exist in B?

4

Grade them

High, medium or low confidence.

965

links proposed: 310 high confidence, 585 medium and 70 low. The whole map runs in about 10 minutes.

The script stores only statistics, no customer data · Dados in Rio · Halloween

This is where the engineering comes in. Since there was no documentation, I let the data speak. For each table, the script finds the key. Then it tests, for each column, whether its values exist in another table, and computes the share that match. The closer to 100%, the higher the confidence. In the end, 965 proposed links, 310 of them high confidence. It stores no customer data, only statistics, and runs in about 10 minutes. Time for a coffee.

Stop 5 · the traps

The same field, several meanings

QTY

In hotels it is nights. In other services it is people.

Cancelled

It is not one status. It is three different codes.

Rows with an asterisk

Extras that do not exist in the catalog and change the room count.

Passengers

The system sums people per row, not real people. To count people, only the booking file record works.

Dados in Rio · Halloween · Nathália Dumit

The map says how tables link, but not what each field means. This is where the business ghosts live. The quantity field is nights for hotels and people for other services. Cancellation has three codes. Some rows with an asterisk are extras outside the catalog, and they change the room count. And the system report sums people per row, which is not the number of passengers. Each of these rules ended up written in code.

Stop 6 · the trial by fire

If it does not match the system report, it is not done

51 of 51

booking files checked one by one against the system's production report: adults and children matched in all of them.

Room nights also reproduce the report month by month. A difference is only accepted once it is explained.

Verified

Dados in Rio · Halloween · Nathália Dumit

The golden rule: no number goes out if it does not match a source the operation already trusts, in this case the system's own production report. To count passengers, I took booking files from one hotel, compared them with the report line by line, and the rule was only accepted when adults and children matched across all 51 files. If a difference exists, it has to be explained, not hidden.

Stop 7 · the lights on

The map produced tools that run by themselves

Single-file dashboard

HTML with the data embedded. Opens on any computer, no server needed.

Excel export

Bookings by file, hotels and agencies month by month, with the screen's filters.

Refresh with a lock

Dados in Rio · Halloween · Nathália Dumit

With the map and the rules in place, the tools stopped being fragile. The dashboard is a single HTML file with the data embedded, so it opens on any machine. It exports to Excel whatever is on screen. And it has a lock: if the database refresh fails, it is not generated with stale data, and the reason stays in the log. Better no dashboard than a lying dashboard.

Stop 8 · “it works on my machine”

The bug that only existed in one folder

The symptom

The Excel file should come out with new tabs. Only four came out.

The cause

The .bat ran an old copy of the script, in another folder.

The proof

The log did not have the new step. Only the log gave the ghost away.

> refresh_dashboard.bat
running: old_folder\\script_v2_final_THISTIME.py
tabs generated: 4 ... where is the rest??

Lesson: one place for the code, and a log that says which version ran.

Dados in Rio · Halloween · Nathália Dumit

A real case. I had delivered the new version of the export, with new tabs, but only four came out. It worked on my machine, of course. I asked for the log: the script that ran was an old copy, in another folder, called by the refresh file. Everyone has had a script_v2_final_THISTIME in their life. The log is what gave the ghost away. Since then: one folder, one version, and the log always records which script ran.

Something that gave me courage: none of the pioneers of computing studied computing, if only because those degrees did not exist. Margaret Hamilton studied Mathematics with Philosophy, learned to program at MIT and led the software of the Apollo missions. Agnes Driscoll combined mathematics with languages to break US Navy codes. Joan Clarke was a cryptanalyst at Bletchley Park, and Cambridge did not even give her the full degree, because she was a woman. Dorothy Vaughan was a teacher and taught herself FORTRAN by reading books. Florence Nightingale did statistics and charts that changed hospitals. Katherine Johnson calculated space trajectories. Mary Jackson had to go to court to be allowed to study and became NASA's first Black female engineer. Here in Brazil, Liane Tarouco was a physicist and wrote, in 1977, the first Brazilian book on data networks; she is the only Brazilian in the Internet Hall of Fame. And Sonia Guimarães, a physicist, was the first Black woman with a PhD in Physics in the country and the first Black professor at ITA. The pattern is clear: a base of reasoning from another field, plus curiosity. In my case, Economics.

8 spells to get there

Spell 1

Start from a pain, not a course

I learned Python because a spreadsheet haunted me every single day.

Spell 2

Read the data before you code

Counts, distincts, nulls. Data is a gossip: it tells everything, you just have to ask.

Spell 3

Be the nitpicker of numbers

Did not match the official report? It is not done. “Almost matches” is a scare postponed.

Spell 4

Bring your baggage

Your old field is not a delay. It is a superpower under another name.

Spell 5

AI is an intern, not an oracle

It writes fast; I am the one who checks and signs off the number.

Spell 6

Leave a trail

Log, version, date. Future you remembers absolutely nothing.

Spell 7

Get the rule out of your head

A rule that lives only in your head goes on vacation with you. Put it in code.

Spell 8

Find your coven

Community, bootcamp, study group. Screaming together is more fun.

Dados in Rio · Halloween · Nathália Dumit

The question I get most: okay, but how do I get there? There is no shortcut, but there are spells. One: start from a real pain, not a course. Two: before writing code, read the data; counts, distincts, nulls. Three: be the nitpicker of numbers; if it does not match an official source, it is not done, and it is only done after QA. Four: bring your baggage; the eye of your previous field is what sets you apart. Five: use AI, I use it a lot, but as an intern; I am the one who checks and signs off. Six: leave a trail, log, version and date, because future you forgets everything. Seven: take the business rule out of your head and put it in code; if it only exists in your memory, that is how a haunted house is born. Eight: find your coven. Nobody does this alone.
